What this does

Every month each tenant owes their share of the building's electricity and water. Doing that math by hand is slow and easy to get wrong. This module does it for you. You type the meter numbers, it makes the bills.

Utility billing run

Bill a whole property at once. This is the usual monthly path.

New bill dialog

Bill one unit, for a late reading or a correction.

Why it matters: bills go out on time, the numbers are right, and tenants get reminded automatically, so you don't lose money to missed payments.

The three dates (and why they differ)

These are easy to mix up, and they answer three different questions:

Period from → to

Which days the usage covers (e.g. Jun 18 → Jul 18). Printed on the tenant's receipt.

Billing month

Which month the bill belongs to. Defaults to this month. You can go back one month, but not forward.

Due date

When the tenant must pay. Set automatically to their own due day, the same as rent.

On the run page the dates are filled in for you and tucked under “Billing period”. Most months you never touch them.

Meter readings & the automatic carry-over

A meter only counts up. What a tenant used = today's number (present) minus last month's (previous):

Present − Previous = Consumed

The part that saves you work: this month's present becomes next month's previous, automatically. You never re-type it.

June bill

Unit 101 · present = 520

July bill

previous = 520 (already there) · present = 590 → used 70

First time for a property? Nothing to carry yet, so Previous shows 0. Type each meter's real previous and present once. From next month, Previous fills itself in.

Two ways to price it, and you pick one

The app needs the price per unit (₱/kWh or ₱/cu.m). Two ways to give it, and you use one at a time, switched by a toggle.

① From total bill

Default

The usual choice. You have the provider's one bill for the whole building but not the per-unit price. The app works it out and splits it by each unit's usage.

Example

  • Provider bill: ₱18,000 for 200 cu.m
  • Rate = 18,000 ÷ 200 = ₱90.00 / cu.m
  • Unit used 24 cu.m → 24 × 90 = ₱2,160.00

② Rate per unit

When you charge a fixed price you set, a flat ₱/kWh or ₱/cu.m, whatever the provider's bill says.

Example

  • Rate you set: ₱13.00 / kWh
  • Unit used: 70 kWh
  • 70 × 13.00 = ₱910.00

You never fill in both. The run page opens on From total bill. Switch to a per-unit rate only when you have a fixed price.

Admin fee, VAT & withholding tax

Three optional add-ons stack on the usage charge. All are percentages and all start at 0. Leave them alone unless they apply to you.

Admin fee: your handling charge, added on top.

VAT: sales tax, added on top. Most residential sub-meters leave this 0.

Withholding tax: taken off the charge. Leave it at 0 unless you withhold.

Worked example: ₱910 usage, admin 10%, VAT 12%, WHT 2%

  • Usage charge (70 kWh × ₱13) = ₱910.00
  • + Admin fee 10% = ₱91.00
  • + VAT 12% = ₱109.20
  • − Withholding 2% = ₱18.20
  • Tenant pays = ₱1,092.00

At 0% the tenant just pays the ₱910 usage charge. Admin fee sits on the main form; VAT and withholding hide under “Taxes (optional)”.

Shortcut, scan the bill: tap “Scan bill photo” and snap the Meralco/Maynilad receipt. The app reads the rate, totals and dates and fills them in. Always double-check before saving. (The photo is sent to an AI service to be read.)

Re-running the same month is safe. Units already billed are skipped, never charged twice, and blank rows are skipped too.

Bill just one unit

For a one-off, like a late reading or a fix, use Bills → New bill instead of a full run:

  1. 1. Pick the lease, then set the type to Electricity or Water.
  2. 2. Switch on meter-reading entry. The previous reading and the rate carry over from the unit's last bill.
  3. 3. Type the present reading. The amount is worked out as you type, so you never enter the peso figure yourself.

Same formulas as the batch run, one bill at a time.
